# VO explainer: narration first, no music, cut on an invisible beat grid

A narrated explainer for an audience that knows nothing about the subject. The voice carries the story. The
picture shows one idea per spoken line, drawn in code (cards, counts, a track of steps, before and after), so a
viewer can follow it with the sound off. There is no music, but the edit keeps the rhythm of a music cut: every
line, cut and reveal lands on a grid nobody hears.

Starter: [starter/](starter/) (`explainer.json` holds the brand, the grid, the voice and the script; see its
[README](starter/README.md) for the exact commands). It runs end to end without a voice or an API key.

## When to use it

| Pick | When |
|---|---|
| **vo-explainer** (this) | Narration is needed, the audience knows nothing, and the subject is a program, a pipeline, a science idea or a concept that has to be explained in order. Length is set by what it takes to be clear, often 2 to 5 minutes. |
| [voice-first-seedance](../voice-first-seedance/RECIPE.md) | Same locked voiceover, but the picture should be illustrated or animated footage generated by a video model (a world, characters, metaphors), not diagrams drawn in code. |
| [vo-synced-legacy](../vo-synced-legacy/starter/) | Rescuing or extending an older film built on `film.json` with word-locked reveals and no beat grid. Do not start new films there. |
| [chapters](../chapters/RECIPE.md) / channel-thread | Release films and catch-ups for people who already know the product. Music, no voice, faster. |

The cue that sends a brief here: "the audience knows nothing", "explain it", "with a voiceover", "no music", or a
previous music-cut film that viewers found unclear.

## The look

It keeps the chapters grammar and slows it down for a first-time viewer:

- **Cold open** with no chapter UI: the pain in a few short lines, each line on screen as the voice says it.
- **Contents page** after the open. Rows land on eighth notes; each chapter row lights up, the camera dives into
  it, and a pullback puts a tick on the row when the chapter ends. The viewer always knows where they are and how
  much is left.
- **One idea on screen per spoken line.** The starter's `show` types: the line itself (`say`), a card, a count, a
  before and after (`swap`), a track of steps with one lit, chips, bars. No side panels, footnotes or chat detours.
- A small screen-space header with the chapter number while inside a chapter. It is never cut by the frame edge.
- **Made-up examples are labelled** on screen (`tag: "example"`). See [truth](../../references/truth.md).
- Brand tokens (palette, fonts, end card) come from the client's own site unless the director names a profile.

## Pace

Numbers from the approved reference (EAP v2, below), from its `SCRIPT-STORYBOARD.md`, `scripts/timeline.py` and the timeline built from the recorded voice:

| | value |
|---|---|
| Speech | about 150 wpm inside a line; 511 words in 72 lines over 5:02, so about 100 wpm across the film |
| Grid | 100 BPM: beat 0.6 s, bar 2.4 s |
| Breath after each line | 0.3 to 0.45 s, plus an extra 0.2 to 0.6 s before a line that starts a new idea. "that is where the ~20% slower pacing comes from" |
| Line starts | on the first half beat after the breath |
| Chapter | 25 to 51 s from landing to exit (six chapters), each ending on a bar line |
| Between chapters | pullback, tick, next row lights, dive: 3.4 to 3.9 s with no voice (6.5 s from the cold open into the contents page) |

The starter uses the same shape: `grid.bpm` 100, `snap_beats` 0.5, `breath` 0.4 s, a per-line `breath` for new
ideas, `min_beats` 3. On its placeholder script it measures about 0.25 camera moves per second, well below the
chapters style's 0.55 to 1.0. That is intended: here a move happens when a new idea is spoken, and the reveals
inside a line (words, chips, bars, a count) keep the frame alive between moves. "Slower" still means holds inside
motion (drift, landing words), never a static slide.

## Sound

- **Voice only. No music bed**, no ambient pad. Soft SFX are allowed unless the director says no: a quiet tick
  under each cut (the starter puts it at -30 dB) and a chime on each chapter tick. `sound.sfx: false` turns them off.
- **The beat grid is invisible.** Nothing plays it. It exists only in the timing: every line starts on a grid
  point, the camera move into a line ends exactly where the voice starts, words of an on-screen line land on the
  eighth note after they are spoken, and chapters close on a bar line. The viewer feels a regular pulse in the
  edit without hearing one. Pick the tempo once (100 BPM in the reference) and never type a second by hand: the
  timing is derived from the recorded line lengths.
- **Record one take per chapter, then cut it into lines** from the take's own character alignment, with 8 ms
  fades at each cut so nothing clicks. Per-line takes sound like separate reads; one take per chapter keeps the
  delivery continuous. Check every word of each take with a local transcription (`scripts/stt.py`) before you
  split, names and respellings first.
- **A one-line fix is a splice, never a regeneration.** Re-record only the changed line (`vo.py line <id>` with
  `"take": "line"` on it) and cut everything else from the approved take exactly as before. Regenerating the whole
  take changes the delivery of every other line, and the director approved that delivery.
- Master to -14 LUFS integrated, true peak at or below -1 dBTP. Captions come from the same line timings
  (`captions.srt`), so they cannot drift.

Details: [sound](../../references/sound.md), [deliverables](../../references/deliverables.md).

## Script rules

Write for someone who arrived by accident ([copy](../../references/copy.md)).

- **Shape: pain, turn, product, plain benefit.** Open on a problem anyone recognises, turn to what was built, show
  how it works in order, end on what it does for the viewer in human terms. Never a README or a feature list.
- **One idea per line, about 3 to 10 words.** Each line is one shot.
- **No jargon.** If a term is needed, say what it does instead ("a search tool", not "an MCP tool"; "takes out the
  names", not "anonymise"; "merges repeats", not "dedupe"). Technical names may appear on screen, not in the voice.
- **Say numbers the way a person says them** ("about seventeen thousand"), and put the exact figure on screen.
- **Strip these every time:**
  - setup then twist ("That turned out to be the hard part.", "Now the uncomfortable part.");
  - tacked-on emphasis at the end of a sentence ("...at all", "...and anyone can check it");
  - a punchline closing each paragraph ("They were right.");
  - signposting ("Here's what...", "Here's why...");
  - "not X but Y" and "isn't X, it's Y" in any disguise, taglines, "Meet...", em dashes;
  - triple fragment beats and stacks of rhetorical questions.
  End paragraphs on a plain fact. Rewriting phrase by phrase keeps the rhythm; do a full plain pass instead.
- **Proof only from signed-off numbers**, and do not claim more than the result says. Label an earlier test as
  earlier. See [truth](../../references/truth.md).
- Spell names phonetically for the voice in `tts_respell`, never on screen.

`scripts/qa.mjs` warns on the mechanical tells (em dashes, signposting, "not just", "honest"). The rhythm tells
need a human read.

## Gates

1. **Brief.** Audience, the one thing they should understand, the structure, the source of every figure.
2. **Script lock.** A script and storyboard document: per chapter, the VO lines, what is on screen per line, word
   counts and estimated seconds at 150 wpm, total runtime, the voice and its character cost. The starter's
   placeholder timing and stills sheets cost nothing, so they can go with it. No voice is recorded before the
   script is approved.
3. **Voice.** Record, transcribe-check, split. Re-run `timing.mjs` and `qa.mjs` on the real durations.
4. **20 s motion preview to the orchestrator**: the cold open into the contents page and the first dive.
5. **Orchestrator frame review** of the preview (stills at 0.25 to 0.5 s steps, plus watching it with sound).
6. **Full render**, mastered.
7. **Review** of the full cut by someone other than its builder: frames pulled from the MP4, with sound.
8. **Deliver.**

## QA

- `node scripts/qa.mjs`: forward and backward seeks are byte-identical; every line starts on the grid; every
  chapter ends on a bar; no line's voice runs into the next; every line holds at least `min_beats`; no text overflows
  its box; copy tells and placeholder timing as warnings.
- `node scripts/stills.mjs 1`: each tile is labelled with the line being spoken. For every tile ask: does the
  picture show the idea in the line, and could someone who knows nothing read it on a phone?
- Sound off test: watch the preview muted. The story should still be followable.
- Sound on test: no clicks at line cuts, no clipped word endings, names pronounced right.
- After mastering: loudness and true peak on the delivered file; captions match the audio.
- Checklists: [qa](../../references/qa.md), [motion](../../references/motion.md).

## Reference films

**Approved: EAP v2** (`~/cs/films/eap-film-v2-20260928`, script approved 2026-09-28). A 5 minute explainer of a
knowledge-extraction program for viewers who know nothing about it. Its `BRIEF.md` asked for "VOICEOVER, NO MUSIC", "edited with the same rhythm/feel as
if it were cut to music", and said "Audience knows nothing. Instructional + explanatory. No jargon, concise, plain
language." Its `TASK.md` turned that into "an invisible beat grid (pick ~100 BPM), and cuts/reveals land on it.
Soft SFX are OK". The approved `SCRIPT-STORYBOARD.md` promises "one idea on screen per spoken line. No chat-thread
detours, footnotes or side chips. A viewer who knows nothing can follow it with the sound off." It explains each
term in words once (a skill is "notes the agent opens by topic") and keeps code names on screen only. Its proof
chapter uses only an audited report and says a search tool "did not make the answers better" where that is what
the numbers showed. The voice was recorded one take per chapter and split by `scripts/vo.py`, with
`scripts/stt.py` checking the pronunciation of a product name. At the time this recipe was written its picture was
still being built, so the starter was built from its script, its VO pipeline and the chapters scaffold, not lifted
from a finished film.

**Rejected after viewer feedback: the Rocco EAP short v2** (`~/cs/films/rocco-short-20260928`, 2:19, delivered 2026-09-28). A
chapters and channel-thread hybrid cut to music at 103 BPM with no voiceover. The EAP v2 `BRIEF.md` records why it
was redone: "current ~2 min cut has too much going on; super unclear what's happening." A music-driven film with
chat threads, side chips and many figures per chapter did not explain the subject to a first-time viewer. This
style exists because of that.

**Rejected: EAP film script v1** (`~/cs/films/eap-deck-blitz-20260927/script-v1.txt`, 2026-09-27). The director
flagged it as reading like Claude wrote it, for lines such as "That turned out to be the hard part.", "...five of them couldn't be
found by name at all." and "...and anyone can check it." The fix that was accepted was a strict plain pass on the
whole script (`script-v2.md`, "strict pass on v1"), not phrase swaps: compare its cold open, "Eight years of client
meetings at PyMC Labs. Seventy-nine clients, six hundred and fifty transcripts, thirty-seven thousand chat
messages. Most of what we learned in those meetings was never written down. So we went back and read all of it."

**Rejected: narrating the deck** (the same folder's `TASK.md`, 2026-09-27). A film concept that walked through a
presentation's slides was found boring: "literally just reading off a script". An explainer is a story with a
pain and a turn, not a slide-by-slide voiceover.

Director-specific taste (a named voice, a house palette, pronunciations, delivery habits) belongs in that
director's profile (`profiles/<name>.md`), not in this recipe.
